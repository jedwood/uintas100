About This Project

I'm on a mission to catch a fish out of at least 100 different Uintas waters. I grew up in Provo fishing and camping all over, but somehow never in the Uintas. At the ripe age of 42 I spent a week on the Highline trail and realized what I've been missing.
"During the original three-phase study, which occurred between 1956 and 1990, biologists surveyed 1,036 lakes. Of this original number of surveyed lakes, 446 lakes are presently being stocked on a prescribed cycle, 129 have sufficient natural reproduction and do not require stocking, and 461 are fishless. Presently 327 lakes are primarily managed for brook trout, 153 with cutthroat trout, 46 with grayling, 35 with tiger trout, 16 with rainbow trout and three lakes with golden trout."
— Utah DWR, Uinta Mountains Lakes Pamphlets (2025 Revised Edition)

Fun Facts

This database tracks 746 lakes across 18 drainages, of which 578 are fishable. Many are stocked regularly, and some were stocked years ago and have established naturally reproducing populations. Another 122 are known to be fishless (some likely never had fish, and some were once stocked until DWR gave up due to frequent winter kill), and 46 more probably have no fish.

Popular Names

One designation appears twice: X-22 exists in both Rock Creek (Pinto) and Swift Creek, so the second is X-22b. A few waters DWR describes but never lettered (Deadfall, Fish Reservoir) carry a JW-n designation of our own.

Stocking Totals

⚠️ Rotenone Treatments

The DWR occasionally treats lakes with rotenone to remove existing fish (usually to restore native cutthroat). Treated lakes are fishless until restocked — check recent stocking records before making the hike.
